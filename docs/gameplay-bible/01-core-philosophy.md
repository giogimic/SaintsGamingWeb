# Saints Gaming — Gameplay Bible (Draft v0.2)

## Game Identity

**Saints Gaming is a creator-driven MMO game engine and platform where players explore, battle, build, and shape the world itself.**

It operates with a dual identity:
1. **The Saints Gaming Engine:** A powerful Babylon.js-driven multiplayer platform supporting skills, inventory, hotbars, MMO social features, object-based worlds, and persistent player systems.
2. **The Tuxemon Reference World:** The first complete, playable game package built on the engine. Tuxemon acts as the compatibility benchmark—if the engine can faithfully run a complete Tuxemon RPG (with its maps, NPCs, story, encounters, creatures, and evolution), the foundation is proven.

The goal is to make the full Tuxemon experience playable inside Saints Gaming, while upgrading it with Saints MMO systems. Tuxemon is not a temporary prototype or throwaway demo. It is the proof of the engine.

The philosophy is simple:
* The engine supports Tuxemon.
* The engine improves Tuxemon.
* The engine eventually creates original experiences beyond Tuxemon.

The defining feature:
**The same tools used to rebuild and enhance Tuxemon are available to players and creators to build entirely new worlds.**

The world is not just consumed. It is built.

# The Core Player Fantasy

*"I can explore a living world, collect powerful creatures, build my own place, develop my skills, play with friends, and eventually create parts of the world myself."*

---

# The Core Gameplay Loop

The loop encompasses both playing existing worlds and creating new ones. The player journey follows this path:
**Explore → Collect → Progress → Build → Create → Share**

## 1. Enter The World

The player begins in the **Lobby / Central Hub**.

The lobby is not a menu. It is a living MMO location.

Players can:
* Meet other players
* Form parties
* Trade
* Show off creatures
* Access skills
* Manage inventory
* Visit player bases
* Access adventures
* Discover community creations

The lobby is the player's home base.

---

# 2. Explore

Players travel into different world zones. This could be existing Tuxemon maps, original Saints areas, or user-generated zones.

Examples:
* Forests
* Caves
* Mountains
* Oceans
* Ruins
* Player-created areas
* Event zones

Each zone contains:
* NPCs and dialogue
* Quests and story progression
* Hidden areas and secrets
* Resources
* Creatures and encounters

---

# 3. Discover & Collect Creatures

Creatures exist in two forms:

## Wild Encounters
Classic creature discovery.
* Grass/bush encounters
* Fishing encounters
* Cave encounters
* Rare environmental encounters

Player finds:
* New creatures
* Variants
* Rare traits
* Evolution paths

## Roaming Monsters
Creatures physically exist in the world.
Players see them, approach them, and engage in combat.

---

# 4. Battle

Combat uses a real-time MMO system, upgrading the traditional creature battle format.

Inspired by:
* RuneScape ability combat
* MMO hotbars
* Creature RPG mechanics

Players have:
* Basic attacks
* Abilities and cooldowns
* Equipment
* Creature abilities
* Party roles

## Party Combat
Supports up to 4 players for cooperative battles, shared encounters, and boss fights with roles like Damage, Support, Control, and Tank.

---

# 5. Progress & Grow Creatures

Captured creatures become part of the player's collection.

## Training
Increase power through battles, experience, and skill interactions.

## Evolution
Creatures evolve through levels, items, conditions, and discovery (supporting the full Tuxemon evolution system).

## Breeding
Players can create new generations with unique traits, abilities, appearance variations, and stats.

---

# 6. Fusion System

A procedural creature system that creates unique combinations without needing millions of handcrafted sprites.
A creature has a base species, traits, abilities, elements, and appearance modifiers. Fusion combines these systems to create unique results with new gameplay purposes.

---

# 7. Develop Skills

Inspired by RuneScape, skills offer long-term progression.
* Gathering (Mining, Woodcutting, Fishing, Farming)
* Crafting (Weapons, Items, Buildings, Decorations)
* Survival (Cooking, Exploration, Creature care)
* Combat (Weapon skills, Creature mastery, Ability progression)

---

# 8. Build Your Territory

Players own customizable spaces—not just houses, but personal worlds.

Players can place buildings, decorate, farm, create production areas, display creatures, and build social spaces using the object-based building system.

Bases progress from simple shelters with storage to advanced bases with workshops, automated resource systems, and community areas.

---

# 9. Create & Share

The creator system is part of the game. The same tools used to rebuild Tuxemon will allow creators to make entirely new games.

Players with permission can:
* Build maps
* Place objects
* Create NPCs
* Design quests
* Customize UI
* Create events

Created content can become private spaces, community areas, public worlds, or official content candidates.

---

# The World System

## Two Map Modes

## Legacy Mode
Purpose: Tuxemon compatibility, fast importing, and existing content support. Uses original tiles, existing maps, and existing structures.

## Object Mode
The future system. Everything becomes an object (e.g., Tree Object with collision, interaction, growth state). The world becomes editable.

---

# The MVP (First Playable Version)

The MVP is not just a small original demo. It must prove the engine can handle a complete RPG experience.

It should prove:
* A multiplayer Saints lobby
* A playable Tuxemon-based area
* Creature encounters
* Combat
* Inventory
* Skills
* Basic editor functionality
* Base system foundation

---

# The Long-Term Vision

Saints Gaming becomes a platform where:
* Developers build worlds
* Players experience worlds
* Creators publish worlds
* The community expands the universe

The goal is not just to recreate existing games, but to combine creature collection, MMO progression, social worlds, and player creation into one connected game.

---

## The Golden Rule

Every feature must answer:
**"Does this make the world feel more alive, more social, or more creative?"**
If not, it waits.
